# Production handoff and review

## Timeline contract

- Distinguish confirmed settings from labeled proposals; use normalized beats or provisional seconds when duration or fps is not confirmed. Do not publish a locked frame plan for unconfirmed settings. An explicitly illustrative assumed-fps example is not a final production schedule. A finite CFR composition uses 0-based frame intervals `[start, end)`: start inclusive, end exclusive. Thus at 30 fps `[0,90)` has 90 frames and ends at 3 seconds; frame 90 starts the next interval.
- Use rational fps values when applicable; do not silently substitute 30 for 29.97. Record the seconds-to-frame rounding policy, transition overlaps, audio offsets and any retiming decisions. Nominal frame counts do not by themselves prove the delivered duration or audio sync.
- A locked sequence needs complete coverage, intentional overlap where specified, no unintended gaps, and a total matching the requested frames. If voice or beat timing is not locked, label frames and timing provisional.
- Define coordinate system and units, intended canvas, actual viewport center, any separate intentional zoom anchor, object groups and their per-group animation/camera rules, z-order and verified asset/caption safe areas. State explicitly whether subheadings are screen-fixed headings or camera-transformed graphics. Describe exact or bounded start/end states and motion paths, readable holds after movement and what persists. For node diagrams, connect lines to intended shapes throughout their visible motion or mark gaps as intentional; avoid implying that illustrative examples are exhaustive or proportional data. A label, another object, subtitle or platform overlay may collide during motion even if the endpoints are clear. Check intermediate frames and time-varying bounds; automated axis-aligned bounding boxes are conservative and cannot catch every visual issue.
- Caption copy and layout may be specified before audio, but cue timings must be labeled provisional until aligned with the recorded narration. Define layout for both one- and multiple-line pages; mark platform-safe area status adjacent to those coordinates. When another file or manifest is authoritative, include it in the deliverable and verify referenced files and duplicate copies match.
- For programmatic rendering, require reproducible seeking for the same frame, inputs, assets and render configuration. Avoid wall-clock-driven animation and uncontrolled randomness; seed intentional randomness. Specific implementation follows the engine's current documentation, and reproducibility is a testable goal, not a guarantee across all hardware and versions. For detailed code-based handoffs/builds follow `../code-video/CONTRACT.md` for an explicit machine-readable rendering interface, authority, profile limitations and scoped QA; no machine-readable file is required for script-only or explicit prose-only requests.

## Review protocol

Review status values: **PASS** (entire named scope checked), **PARTIAL** (some but not all of that scope checked), **FAIL** (observed defect), **NOT CHECKED** (no relevant evidence), **NEEDS HUMAN REVIEW** (human judgment or sign-off required), **NOT APPLICABLE** (reason stated). Name the precise scope of each pass—for example, selected text bounds—not an untested overall geometry, motion or accessibility pass. Selected-frame checks alone imply PARTIAL for all-frame geometry. Cite evidence, and do not infer what was not observed. Separate exact visual frame math under stated assumptions from unverified audio timing, caption sync and platform safety.

1. Compare objective, promise, story and visible evidence against the brief. Look at the opening and payoff and ask whether a stranger has enough context.
2. Inspect decoded output stills at scene start, end, either side of cuts, after settling, mid-motion and any potential collisions, including text and overlays. Sampling stills cannot establish that every frame is correct; use continuous geometry checks when available and watch playback.
3. Watch the full motion at normal speed; use slower playback around defects. Check pacing, reading time, jitters and transitions; a contact sheet alone is not a full playback review.
4. Listen to the actual final mix, check VO/music/SFX synchronization and speech clarity, and inspect synchronized captions including relevant non-speech sounds. If absent, distinguish intentional silent design from untested audio.
5. Check factual claims, source and asset permissions, relevant visual-description needs, actual contrast against changing backgrounds, readable text, flashing/motion safety and each actual platform placement/aspect ratio. Guidance is contextual; no self-declared compliance without evidence.
6. Verify file dimensions, duration, fps, frame count and required audio/caption streams against the confirmed delivery specification.
7. Prioritize actionable defects as blocker, major or minor using the definitions and evidence rules in `operating-contract.md`. Report scene and time/frame, observed evidence, intended behavior, proposed repair and affected dependencies. After a repair, check the changed range and neighboring scenes: a fix may introduce a new collision elsewhere.

Read `operating-contract.md` for exact minimum sampling, reading-hold, contrast, frame, integrity, priority and revision procedures; `platform-sources.md` provides placement-specific source checklist.
