# Integrated voice, picture and sound direction

Use this when a brief asks for music-led edits, voice-synchronized kinetic typography, cinematic audio or a reference analysis. A reference is a source of principles and a quality bar, not permission to copy its script, signature visual motif, music or shot sequence. A user-supplied or model-generated analysis is **reported**, not independently verified. Specific EQ bands, compressor settings, ducking amounts, exact timestamps and "frame-perfect" claims require direct signal measurement or a human listening/viewing review; describe an unverified inference as such.

## Plan an integrated performance

Design the narrative and emotional arc before individual effects. Adapt hook, contrast, tension, release, proof and resolution to the available duration and topic; do not mechanically transplant a long reference's two-act structure to a short clip. Map each beat to its purpose and truth/qualifier, VO phrase and intended emphasis, focal visual and camera movement, type choreography, music state and timed effects. Leave intelligible holds and pauses; visual intensity is not a substitute for comprehension. The entire spoken story should remain truthful without the picture, and captions should retain qualifiers.

Generate or record the actual approved VO first. Preserve a clean master and describe the voice/processing honestly: a selected TTS voice is not a human performance and does not prove it has a particular microphone, vocal timbre or artistic quality. Build a cue sheet from actual recording time, not rough script word counts. Capture each cue's source, clock units, start/end, uncertainty, tolerance and how it was validated. Prefer audio-derived word/phoneme alignment or manual listening correction; an automatic aligner is an estimate, not proof of syllable-exact boundaries. Resolve transcript mismatches and mark uncertain words for human review. Distinguish phrase-level alignment from word-level and syllable-level alignment. Converting seconds to frames quantizes events; document rounding and frame rate. Provide readable phrase-level captions even if punch words animate individually.

Design a few **intentional sync moments**, not an arbitrary effect on every syllable. For each: record VO target, intended visual trigger, effect transient, music beat and any deliberate offset. A hit can coincide in the timeline while still being masked, early/late perceptually or misaligned with the actual speech; validate after encoding and listening. For beat matching use the actual beat grid and choose/correct edit timings in relation to it; do not claim beat sync just because music is present. Preserve meaningful whitespace and avoid epileptogenic flashing and excessive camera shake.

## Sound and mix

Specify a suitable original/cleared music bed (style, pulse, changes and ending) plus an effects palette that serves this specific narrative; do not copy a reference's proprietary track or signature motif. Assign transition, emphasis and UI micro-sounds to visible actions, and provide meaningful rests. Separate VO, effects and music buses so their levels can be controlled independently. Music changes, drops or silence must have a designed duration and respect the VO, not randomly interrupt it.

If ducking is requested, apply measured/enveloped or compressor-based ducking to the **music bus driven by VO**, document threshold/attack/release/range and whether it is automatic; don't claim a specific dB reduction without measuring gain. Optionally use EQ, de-essing and compression appropriate to the recording rather than prescribing a single frequency recipe or imitating a voice. Do not imply these processes were applied if they were not. Balance effects below intelligible narration, inspect clipping/true peak and integrated loudness against the actual delivery requirement if specified, and use an appropriate limiter only when it is needed. The presence of sidechain, EQ or numerical peak headroom is not proof of a good mix. Human listening on headphones and phone speakers remains necessary.

## Acceptance and evidence

Review the encoded export, not only source cues: test schema and timeline consistency, inspect sample transitions and cue targets, report measured technical offsets and encoding frame quantization, analyze final mix/headroom, and review subtitles. Listen to the whole piece and watch it at 1× with audio if available. Record which cue timings were automatically estimated, manually verified, or NOT CHECKED; identify unmeasured beat alignment, loudness/true peak, intelligibility, subjective quality and platform overlays separately. If continuous playback or listening is unavailable, those areas and creative acceptance are NEEDS HUMAN REVIEW; don't assert frame-perfect alignment or reference-level quality. Keep before/after evidence for any revised collision or timing defect and recheck neighboring moments.
