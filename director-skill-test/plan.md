# Proposed Director skill: test briefs and plans

These are synthetic briefs prepared for this evaluation. They are not client claims or published videos. The same assistant authored the plans and assessed them; this is a self-test, not an independent or controlled benchmark. No skill was installed.

## Test A — Short educational explainer (the rendered sample)

**Brief:** Explain a simple first-in, first-out queue with one consumer to a beginner in a muted, six-second vertical social video. Text-only; no brand, CTA, narration or sound. 360 × 640 px, 30 fps, exactly 180 frames. These dimensions are for a test preview, not a platform recommendation. No platform placement specified; platform overlay compliance remains unverified.

**Viewer promise:** See that three numbered items wait in order and the first one is served first. This is a simplified model, not a claim about every queue.

**Visual direction:** Dark navy background; warm near-white text; numbered turquoise circles. Stable centered hierarchy, large text, no decorative background. The queue is a rounded rectangular container; place its label below and right-aligned with the container, clear of moving circles throughout the timeline. Keep all important elements at least 48 px from the left/right edges. This is a project margin, not an asserted platform safe zone.

**Motion direction:** Hard cuts between scenes; within scenes use simple, controlled motion. First settle items into a queue, then move R1 to a consumer. 0-based, start-inclusive, end-exclusive ranges; no scene overlap.

| Scene | Frames | Purpose and visual | Motion / sound |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | [0,60) | Title `REQUESTS ARRIVE`; R1, R2, R3 arranged left-to-right above empty queue | All objects hold still. Silent. |
| 2 | [60,120) | Title `WAIT IN ORDER`; same numbered items line up inside queue | R1, R2, R3 move together from y=180 to y=320 during frames 60–89; hold frames 90–119. Silent. |
| 3 | [120,180) | Title `FIRST IN, FIRST OUT`; R2/R3 wait; R1 moves to consumer labeled `SERVED` | R1 moves from (100,320) to (180,460) during frames 120–149; hold 150–179. Silent. |

**Handoff:** Origin top-left, positions in preview pixels. Layer order: background → queue and consumer → request circles → labels and titles. Titles are centered and may wrap to two lines. Consumer is an outlined box beneath the queue. A hard cut changes the scene labels at frames 60 and 120; circles retain labels and colors. No image, video, music, user data, or third-party brand assets. Font: DejaVu Sans Bold from the local system. No captions track is required for this deliberately silent test; on-screen copy communicates all of its content. The plan is not a complete accessibility certification.

**Review points:** Frames 0, 59, 60, 89, 90, 119, 120, 149, 150, 179; full playback; check on-screen text fits, frame boundaries, first item served, exact output duration and dimensions. Audio and sync are not tested because the sample intentionally has no audio.

## Test B — Fictional product demonstration

**Brief:** A 15-second, 1080 × 1920, 30 fps product video for YouTube Shorts. Audience: freelancers. Supplied product fact: an imaginary app called TaskNest lets users group tasks into projects. No supplied UI, logo, metrics, customer stories, music or CTA. Text-only. Assumed tone: practical and calm. No claim beyond the supplied fact. The name and concept are test fixtures, not a real product endorsement.

**Promise:** See how grouping tasks by project makes the structure of a task list clearer. **Hook options:** (1) `Too many tasks, no clear groups?` (2) `One list. Two projects.` Recommended: option 2, because it directly demonstrates the supplied feature without promising improved productivity.

**Visual:** Light neutral background, dark text, blue accent; mock diagram clearly labeled `Illustration`, not a product screenshot. Simple rectangles represent tasks; use two large labeled columns only if a phone-size preview shows them readable, otherwise stack them. Exact UI and platform safe areas remain unverified.

| Frames | Storyboard |
|---|---|
| [0,90) | Show four task labels in one neutral list; title `One list. Two projects.` Hold long enough to read. |
| [90,210) | Form two labeled containers, `Project A` and `Project B`; task cards move into them one at a time. Visual demonstrates grouping, not an invented app interaction. |
| [210,360) | Hold grouped arrangement; text `Group tasks by project`. Clearly label the graphics `Illustration`. |
| [360,450) | Resolve to `TaskNest — group tasks by project.` No CTA. |

**Review:** Check exact copy, phone-size legibility, the illustration label, no invented interface or statistics, correct 450-frame duration, transitions, and actual placement safe areas when supplied. No audio is planned.

## Test C — Data story with unavailable source

**Brief:** 20-second vertical social video about how a hypothetical monthly figure changed. The requester says “show a 40% increase” but provides no baseline, end value, source, period or permission to use fictional data. 1080 × 1920, 30 fps. No audience specified.

**Expected intake behavior:** Stop before writing a claim or chart. Ask: (1) What are the source, values, period and units for the claimed 40% increase? (2) Who is the audience, and is the figure real or explicitly illustrative? Without answers, the number must not appear as a verified claim. We did not fabricate a chart, source or outcome.

**What can still be planned after clarification:** A setup → comparison → contextual explanation → conclusion structure. Chart encoding and scale depend on the actual values. Accessible labels and a descriptive alternative should be planned. This is an intake/guardrail test, not a completed storyboard.

## Test D — Long-form YouTube opening

**Brief:** Plan the first 45 seconds of a 10-minute educational YouTube video for non-specialists: `Why do bridges expand in heat?` 16:9, 1920 × 1080, 30 fps. No bridge-specific measurements, supplied footage or branded assets. Single narrator; exact recorded timing not yet available. Goal: explain the concept, not promote a product.

**Promise:** Explain why bridge structures allow for temperature-related expansion. **Hook options:** (1) `Why does a bridge need gaps?` (2) Show two ends of a stylized bridge moving slightly toward a joint. Recommended: combine the question with the visual; it demonstrates the promised topic without implying a specific bridge is unsafe.

**Direction:** Use a restrained, labeled 2D schematic with exaggerated movement identified as illustrative. Dark text on light background, limited warm/cool accents. Exact geometry and measurements are not represented as real. Full-video payoff and title/thumbnail need confirmation with the larger production.

| Provisional time | Scene direction |
|---|---|
| 0–8 s | Introduce the question over a simplified bridge joint. Hold the key label still; a subtle warm color sweep suggests heat. VO: `Why does a bridge need gaps?` |
| 8–20 s | Show a schematic section lengthening toward the joint. VO: `Many materials expand as their temperature rises.` Movement is exaggerated and labeled. |
| 20–32 s | Show the gap narrowing without the sections colliding. VO: `A joint can give a structure room to move.` |
| 32–45 s | Introduce the next question: `How do engineers plan for that movement?` Hold the diagram; continue into the main explanation. Exact VO sync and frame ranges remain TBD until narration is recorded. |

**Review:** Confirm the scientific wording with an appropriate source or subject expert, flag that the schematic is not a specific bridge design, check title/thumbnail agreement, VO and captions after recording, diagram legibility, and the full video's actual payoff. No claim of scientific verification has been made in this test.
